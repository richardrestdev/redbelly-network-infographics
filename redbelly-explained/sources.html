02 Why tokenise real world assets?

Printed source line: tokeniser.com / Redbelly whitepaper / TASK-07

What the infographic saysPageSupporting quote
Tokenisation records ownership as digital tokens and lets the asset's rules be programmed.Tokeniser website“Tokenisation records ownership of financial products and services as digital tokens, and lets you program the rules of the asset.”
Today: separate systems to reconcile; a (corporate) bond trade can take 2 to 4 business days to settle.TASK-07 Project Acacia deep dive (winning entry)“take two to four business days to settle, because the asset record, the payment instruction, and the legal documentation typically live on different systems operated by different institutions”
Tokenised: one shared record.Redbelly Network website“Establish digital registries as a shared source of truth across issuers, managers, and investors.”
Tokenised: one record shared by every party in the value chain.Redbelly whitepaper (PDF, 28 Aug 2025)“using a verifiable, single source of truth across the entirety of the value chain”
Tokenised: asset and payment swap in one step.TASK-07 Project Acacia deep dive (winning entry)“a single atomic transaction can exchange both simultaneously”
Today: manual checks, breaches often found late (after the trade).Tokeniser: automated compliance“Investor eligibility, holder limits and transfer restrictions are typically managed through spreadsheets, emails and manual sign-offs. Breaches are often identified after the trade”
Tokenised: checked before every trade.Tokeniser: automated compliance“each trade is checked against your fund's rules before execution”
Tokenised: lower investment thresholds.Redbelly whitepaper (PDF, 28 Aug 2025)“lowering investment thresholds”
Tokenised: fractions (fractional ownership), traded 24/7.Tokeniser website“Holders gain fractional ownership, direct custody, atomic settlement, and 24/7 secondary trading through protocol finance.”

03 DBFT consensus

Printed source line: redbelly.network/whitepaper / redbelly.network/research

What the infographic saysPageSupporting quote
DBFT stands for Democratic Byzantine Fault Tolerant consensus.Redbelly whitepaper (PDF, 28 Aug 2025)“Democratic Byzantine Fault Tolerant (DBFT) consensus”
DBFT is leaderless.Redbelly whitepaper (PDF, 28 Aug 2025)“As a leaderless consensus algorithm, it eliminates single points of failure”
Classical blockchains: nodes compete, one block wins, the rest is wasted.Redbelly whitepaper (PDF, 28 Aug 2025)“This is a waste of resources as only one participant succeeds and the rest fail.”
DBFT: nodes cooperate and combine transactions into a superblock.Redbelly whitepaper (PDF, 28 Aug 2025)“combining distinct sets of transactions into a ‘superblock’ in a leaderless manner”
Redbelly consensus is collaborative instead of competitive.Redbelly whitepaper (PDF, 28 Aug 2025)“Redbelly consensus is collaborative instead of competitive”
No leader: every node plays a similar role.Research: DBFT, Efficient Leaderless Byzantine Consensus“each node plays a similar role in the execution of the consensus”
No forks: once decided, a block is final.Redbelly whitepaper (PDF, 28 Aug 2025)“It cannot fork and has been mathematically proven to be correct through a process of formal verification through parameterised model checking”
Fault tolerant: proven (by model checking) safe while fewer than 1 in 3 nodes are faulty.Research: Holistic Verification of Blockchain Consensus“for any number n of processes and any number f < n/3 of Byzantine processes”
Proven: the verification covers safety (and liveness) for any parameter.Research: Holistic Verification of Blockchain Consensus“we formally verify, for any parameter, not only the safety properties of the Redbelly Blockchain consensus but also its liveness”

06 Zero knowledge proofs

Printed source line: docs.redbelly.network: selective disclosure vs ZK

What the infographic saysPageSupporting quote
A ZK proof shows something is true without revealing the data behind it.Redbelly docs: Selective disclosure vs zero knowledge“uses zero-knowledge proofs, allow users to prove a statement is true without revealing any of the underlying data that makes it true”
Ask: an app asks one yes or no question (example on the artwork: over 18?).Redbelly docs: Selective disclosure vs zero knowledge“The verifier asks a yes/no question using a query, like “Is the user over 18?””
Prove: your wallet proves it (generates the proof); nothing is revealed.Redbelly docs: Selective disclosure vs zero knowledge“The user's wallet generates a cryptographic proof that they satisfy the query, without revealing their address or any other personal information.”
Artwork: the passport credential's fields stay hidden; the birth date is queried, not revealed.Redbelly docs: Proof by Query“can be queried against a relative date, rather than being revealed”
Check: a smart contract on Redbelly verifies it.Redbelly docs: Selective disclosure vs zero knowledge“where a smart contract verifies its validity”
Artwork: the result is true or false, with no personal data on chain.Redbelly docs: Selective disclosure vs zero knowledge“with no personal data ever being exposed on-chain”
Selective disclosure reveals only the fields asked for.Redbelly docs: Selective disclosure vs zero knowledge“Selective Disclosure (SD) is a method where a user reveal only specific fields from their credential to a verifier.”
Zero knowledge reveals no data, only the proof.Redbelly docs: Selective disclosure vs zero knowledge“In SD, data is revealed. In PBQ, only the proof is shared”
